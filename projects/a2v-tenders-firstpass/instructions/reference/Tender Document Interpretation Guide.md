# Tender Document Interpretation Guide

## Purpose

This document helps the AI correctly interpret tender documents before searching for historical references.

Tender documents often contain large amounts of information. The AI must extract the most important details and ignore irrelevant content.

The goal is to identify the key characteristics of the product being requested.

These characteristics will guide the search for historical tenders.

---

## Common Tender Document Sources

Most tenders analysed by A2V originate from:

retail clients like **ALDI**

These tenders are typically delivered through the **ARIBA procurement system**.

Users will usually download multiple files from ARIBA, which may include:

* Product specification documents
* Pricing templates
* Technical drawings
* Packaging specifications
* Sample instructions
* Submission deadlines

Not all documents contain useful information for tender analysis.

---

## Key Information to Extract

When reviewing tender documents, the AI should extract the following information where possible.

### Product Name

The product name is the most important search identifier.

Examples:

Heavy Duty Tape Dispenser
Craft Scissors Set
Paint Brush Pack

This should be used when searching historical projects.

---

### Product Description

Tender documents often include additional descriptive text about the product.

Examples may include:

* material specifications
* size or dimensions
* functionality
* packaging type

This information helps determine whether historical products are similar.

---

### Product Category

The product category may be explicitly stated or inferred.

Examples:

Craft
Stationery
Packaging tools
Seasonal products

If the category is not clearly stated, the AI should infer it based on the product description.

---

### Retail Price

Retail price may be provided in the tender documents or referenced in related materials.

Retail price is an important benchmark when comparing historical tenders.

If available, extract:

* retail price
* currency
* pricing expectations

---

### Estimated Quantity

Some tenders specify estimated order volumes.

Examples:

100,000 units
250,000 units

Estimated quantity can indicate the commercial scale of the opportunity.

---

### Region

The tender may apply to specific regions.

Examples:

UK
IB
EU

This information may influence packaging or regulatory requirements.

---

### Submission Deadline

Tender documents often include a submission deadline.

Example:

Samples required by Wednesday
Final submission deadline Friday

This information can affect feasibility.

---

### Sample Requirements

Tender documents may specify whether samples are required.

Examples:

Existing product sample
New sample required
Prototype required

Sample requirements can significantly affect preparation effort.

---

### Packaging Requirements

Packaging format is often specified in tender documents.

Examples include:

Blister packaging
PDQ display
Window box

Packaging requirements may affect manufacturing complexity.

---

## Identifying the Core Product Request

Tender documents may contain a large amount of background information.

The AI should focus on identifying the **core product request**.

Example:

If the tender document describes packaging requirements, compliance rules, and logistics details, the AI should still identify the primary product being requested.

Example interpretation:

"The tender appears to request a heavy duty tape dispenser product intended for retail sale through ALDI."

This concise interpretation helps guide the historical search.

---

## Handling Multiple Product Variants

Some tenders may include multiple related products.

Examples:

Three variations of the same product
Multiple sizes of the same item

In these cases, the AI should:

1. Identify the main product category.
2. Treat each variation as part of the same overall product family.

Historical projects involving similar product families may still be relevant.

---

## Handling Incomplete Information

Tender documents may sometimes be incomplete.

If certain information is missing, the AI should:

* proceed with available information
* note the missing fields
* avoid making assumptions

Example:

If retail price is not specified, the AI should search for historical retail benchmarks instead.

---

## Output Expectations

After reviewing tender documents, the AI should provide a brief interpretation of the tender.

This interpretation should include:

Product name
Product description
Product category
Retail positioning (if known)
Estimated quantity (if available)
Submission timeline (if available)

This summary becomes the starting point for historical research.

---

## Important Reminder

The AI must avoid drawing conclusions about whether A2V should pursue the tender.

Its role is to interpret the documents and gather supporting historical context.

The final decision always belongs to the A2V team.
